1How the data was made, every step

Every step, and the check behind it.

The story page tells the making of in eight steps. This page keeps everything behind them: each source we tried, each rule, and the number each test gave. The full lab notebook, with every script, is in the repository (WebScrape/PROCESS.md and Enrichment/README.md).

The brief, and every source we tried

The assignment asked for every publication of IIT Kharagpur and IIT Roorkee from 2016 to 2026, with title, authors, year, type, venue, ISSN, DOI, citations and references. We set one rule before writing any code: a value we cannot establish is written as TODO, never guessed.

What this decidedNo single source covers everything. Recall had to come from the union of several independent sources, and quality from an evidence trail on every record: which source found it, and what confirmed it.

Finding the papers: by place and by person

Two kinds of search ran side by side. The first looks for the IIT in the author affiliations printed on papers. The second starts from the faculty and lists their papers. They catch different papers, so we kept both and merged them.

Search by place: rare words, judged locally

Crossref treats a multi-word query as "any of these words", so "IIT KGP" returns about 21,000 mostly unrelated hits. We searched on single rare tokens instead (city names, PIN codes and misspellings) and did all filtering ourselves. For a rare token the search behaves like an exact filter: for "Ropar", 3,481 of the 3,482 hits had the word in an author affiliation.

Broad sweeps on the state names (West Bengal, 277,583 records, and Uttarakhand, 18,267) catch strings that name the IIT without the city. After the classifier fix described below, the West Bengal sweep added only 3 Kharagpur records beyond the targeted words. That is our evidence that the word list is complete for Crossref.

Full Crossref records came back at about 100 per 40 seconds. Asking only for the DOI, authors, title, type and date gave 1,000 per 50 seconds, so the search used these light records and the full metadata was fetched afterwards, 40 DOIs per request.

The classifier: what does "IIT Kharagpur" look like?

Each author's affiliation text is put into one of four classes, by these rules in order.

  1. No city, PIN code or e-mail domain of the IIT appears: no decision.
  2. A fuzzy "Indian Institute of Technology" or IIT pattern, or a unit name of this IIT, appears: the IIT. The match is fuzzy because real strings contain typos such as Indian Institue, Tehcnology, Instituteof and "Indi | an Institute".
  3. A known other organisation in the same town appears: not the IIT. For Kharagpur this covers the local colleges, the South Eastern Railway, IIM, IIEST, KGP Bremerhaven (a German clinic) and Kazakh "KGP" health centres. For Roorkee it covers the National Institute of Hydrology, CSIR-CBRI, COER, Quantum, Patanjali, CPPRI and others.
  4. A department, school or centre name together with the city, and no other organisation: the IIT, because it is the only multi-department research institution there. This rule is not used for Saharanpur, which has many unrelated institutions.
  5. Anything else: uncertain. The record is kept and marked TODO.

Every distinct string that mentioned the city but was not accepted was read by a person. For Kharagpur that was 78 strings, of which 11 stayed uncertain after the rules were refined. For Roorkee, 293 strings were reviewed and 49 stayed uncertain (67 author slots). What is left are bare places such as "Kharagpur, West Bengal, India", which nobody can decide.

A bug the review caughtA pattern added for the broken string "Indi | an Institute" was too loose and matched any "Indian Institute…", including "Indian Institute of Science, Kharagpur". Reading the strings by hand found it.

A second one, from the broad sweepsThe state sweeps first "found" 650 extra Kharagpur records. They were false: generic unit names such as "School of Energy Science and Engineering" matched universities in Harbin and Tianjin. Generic unit names now count only together with the city.

Records are joined on the normalised DOI, else on the title and year. A record without a DOI whose normalised title matches a DOI record (year within one) is merged into it. Crossref is the main metadata source, DataCite covers DOIs that Crossref does not hold (arXiv and Zenodo, for example), and the channel's own metadata is the last fallback.

Preprints that Crossref links to a published version, or that have an identical title, are merged into the published paper: 1,622 at Kharagpur and 752 at Roorkee. Twin registrations of the same work were merged too: 349 and 127. Peer reviews, datasets, journal issues, standards and similar records are not papers and are left out.

Checking with the text printed on the paper

Two sources were used only to confirm or refute records, by reading real affiliation text:

  • Europe PMC, looked up by DOI in batches: 3,622 of 45,861 DOIs had affiliations there.
  • Springer article pages, which carry an institution tag for each author. We used an honestly named user agent, only the paths that robots.txt allows, and at most one request per second for each of three workers.

ScienceDirect (HTTP 403), IEEE Xplore (a challenge page), Wiley, Taylor & Francis, AIP and ACS (HTTP 403) were tested and left alone. No bot check was bypassed anywhere.

Later, OpenAlex's raw affiliation text was read by the same classifier, under the same rule. A likely record became verified only when an author's affiliation text on the paper named the IIT, and it was rejected when every author's affiliation named another institution.

Measuring what we missed, and what we got wrong

Recall and precision pull in opposite directions. Looser matching finds more real papers and more wrong ones. We measured both, against data the pipeline never saw.

The precision audit: the Kumar problem

For each institute we drew a random sample with a fixed seed (20261007): 25 verified, 40 likely and 40 possible records. A separate agent checked each one against the affiliation printed on the paper, using Crossref, Europe PMC, Springer page tags, open-access PDFs and arXiv PDFs.

Kharagpur was audited before the namesake fixes and Roorkee on the final data, where 32 of 40 likely records could not be checked because the publisher sites block scripted access. Precision by the channel that found an unverified record (correct / wrong, both audits): DBLP persons 0 / 6, undated ORCID 3 / 12, dated ORCID 20 / 7, roster ORCID 10 / 3, Crossref author search 21 / 32.

The failures had three causes: namesakes with common names (Kumar, Sharma, Singh, Das, Ghosh), papers from before a person joined or after they left, and ORCID or DBLP profiles of a different person. What we changed:

  1. The main file holds verified records and well-supported likely ones. Everything weaker goes to a separate file with the reason, so nothing is thrown away and nothing weak is analysed.
  2. Co-author identities use the surname and the full first name, because surname plus initial collided massively.
  3. For common surnames (carried by 8 or more different first names in the institute's own verified papers, 410 at Kharagpur), an initial alone such as "N. Kumar" is never matched to a person.
  4. A name-ambiguity guard: if a name is much more common in Crossref than the person's verified papers explain, only ORCID or affiliation evidence, or two shared co-authors plus a shared venue, is accepted.
  5. The snowball was removed from the main file. In one round, one common-name profile grew from 325 to 424 seed papers by taking in its own partly wrong matches.
  6. The Europe PMC and Springer checks described above were added to confirm or refute records with real affiliation text.

The trade-off, recorded honestlyRemoving the snowball lowered recall on the Ropar pilot's golden set from 85.5% to 76.1%. Most of the difference sits in the separate file. In exchange, a large number of namesake errors left the dataset.

Seven defects, fixed in the code

Each defect the check found was fixed in the pipeline and the files rebuilt. None was patched by hand in the output.

  1. About 300 records had lost their Crossref metadata after an early failed batch. The retry logic was fixed, with a single-DOI retry for batch misses.
  2. Duplicate registrations of one work (ASME twin DOIs, the German edition of Angewandte, Inderscience twins, IOP suffixes, several preprint versions) were merged, and the other DOIs kept. A conference paper and its later journal version stay two papers.
  3. Publisher reference lists that cite the paper itself were cleaned.
  4. INSPIRE hits with no IIT author affiliation became weak evidence only.
  5. Years delivered as text were converted to numbers.
  6. ISSNs from the journal-title lookup were normalised to the XXXX-XXXX form.
  7. DOIs of supplementary material were excluded.

Resolving TODOs without guessing

Open fields were filled only from a source record: a single-DOI retry in Crossref, doi.org content negotiation (works for any DOI agency), a title search in Crossref, DataCite and Semantic Scholar for records without a DOI, the ISSN from Crossref's journal registry by exact title, and Semantic Scholar's reference lists and counts as a last fallback. A title match was accepted only with title-word overlap of at least 0.9, a year within one and, when the authors are known, a shared author surname. It found 314 DOIs for Kharagpur and 175 for Roorkee. At Kharagpur, missing venues fell from 1,356 to 118, missing reference lists from 5,205 to 3,929 and missing DOIs from 1,072 to 928.

Enrichment, scope and faculty links

With an API key, OpenAlex filled what Crossref lacked. Then the scope was set, the ranks attached and every paper linked to the faculty who wrote it.

OpenAlex fills gaps and never overwrites

OpenAlex gave the work record for every DOI (100 per request), a title search for records without a DOI, the DOIs of cited works where the references were missing, and the Indian works that our papers cite and that cite them. Existing values were never overwritten. Only fields still marked TODO were filled, and every filled field records its source in the record.

A check script compares every enriched record with the original. It confirms that the records and their order are unchanged, that no original value changed except the documented ones, that every filled value is logged (and every logged one filled), that the open-field list is exact, that quartiles sit only on journal papers and come from the Scimago file of the publication year, that CORE ranks sit only on conference papers, and that the Indian-citation flags agree with the institutions listed. Re-run on 29 October 2026: all checks passed for all 33,393 Kharagpur and 25,775 Roorkee records.

Ranks

A journal's quartile comes from Scimago by exact ISSN, using the file for the year of publication (2026 papers use the 2025 file). Scimago's site blocks scripts, so the ten yearly files were downloaded by hand. A conference's rank comes from the CORE edition in force that year: CORE2018 up to 2019, then CORE2020, CORE2021 for 2021–22, CORE2023 for 2023–25 and ICORE2026. A venue matches a CORE entry by name, or by its acronym together with overlapping name words, plus a short list of exact names checked by hand.

Scope: science and engineering

After the professor's review on 12 October we kept only papers whose OpenAlex topic is in the Physical Sciences or Life Sciences. This removed 4,069 papers at Kharagpur and 2,518 at Roorkee. Faculty of humanities, management, law, education and medical units are not reported, but their science papers still count for the institute.

The network files

A last script checks the three network files against the brief and against the dataset: required columns, no self-citations or duplicate pairs, both ends of an internal edge in the submission, the cross-institute flag right on every edge, and the citation flags in the paper file consistent with the edges. All network checks passed for 86,090 internal citation edges, 185,184 citations from and 218,595 citations to other Indian institutions' works, and 137,473 author pairs.

Checking the analysis

No number went into the report until it was counted a second way. These are the checks behind the charts on the story page and in the full chapters.

  • Faculty-to-faculty citations counted by a matrix product and by a loop: 50,372 both times.
  • Co-author distances against networkx (0 differ), and the bow-tie of the citation network by networkx and by SciPy (the same).
  • Papers with authors from several departments recounted from the submitted CSV (3,265 both times), and department paper counts against the department table (0 differ).
  • The professor's tables recounted cell by cell from the submitted CSV.
  • A second run of every new script: all 19 result files identical, because random rewiring now uses sorted edges and fixed seeds.
  • The profile explorer's builder recomputes papers, citations, h-index, i10-index and Q1 papers for all 1,410 faculty and refuses to write unless every value equals the faculty table: 0 mismatches. It found 8 DOIs that appear twice in OpenAlex (online-first year and issue year), and Crossref decided which record to keep.

  • Every new reference in the report was checked on Crossref or PubMed.

What the checks and the data overturned

Expected: PageRank finds the landmark papersFound: it finds old papers cited by their own authors. 88% of internal citations are self-citations, so we added a PageRank without them and one rescaled by age.

Expected: a hub-dominated, "scale-free" networkFound: a lognormal fits citations better than a power law. Preferential attachment was measured directly, not assumed.

Expected: a robust collaboration webFound: it is robust to random loss and fragile to targeted loss. Removing 7% of brokers halves it.

The rules behind the numbers

The fine print that every chart on the story page and in the full chapters follows.

Journal and conference ranks
A journal's quartile is Scimago's for the year the paper came out, and a conference's rank is from the CORE edition in force that year. A paper is never judged by a rank its venue earned later.
Impact factor
IF here is OpenAlex's two-year mean citedness, which runs higher than Clarivate's JIF. Citation counts are as of October 2026, so recent papers have had less time.
Influence and communities
PageRank uses a damping of 0.85, and is also run without self-citations and rescaled by age. Communities come from Louvain over ten random seeds, checked with Leiden. Robustness and SIR spreading are simulated on the faculty network.
Beyond pairs
Katz, communicability and HITS are computed from the adjacency matrix. Scores that are zero outside one part of the network are compared only where they are non-zero. Hypergraph and trio results use papers with a linked professor and at most 50 authors.
Two layers
Professor-to-professor citations use internal citations only; under the strict rule the two papers share no author. Pairs are compared with pairs of the same kind (department relation, subfield, output), and network results with random networks of the same degrees. FWCI is OpenAlex's.
Predicting departments
Department strengths use OpenAlex subfields. The test of whether a department moves into related areas predicts 2021–26 using only 2016–20 data.

Things that broke

Each incident and its fix is written down in the lab notebook, so it does not happen again.

  • A pkill command matched its own shell and killed itself twice. Processes are now found by an anchored pattern and stopped by their id.
  • Two writers appended to one compressed file and corrupted it. Caches that grow across runs are now plain line files, readers accept a cut-off last line, and a script checks the compressed caches.
  • A laptop suspend left network calls hanging for three hours. The jobs were restarted, and every cache resumes where it stopped.
  • arXiv blocked two processes running at once, so arXiv now runs as one process at one request every five seconds.
  • Malformed DOIs from ORCID ("1-25", "doi 10.…") broke Crossref batch requests. DOIs are now cut to the 10.xxxx/… pattern and anything else is dropped.
  • A usage limit stopped all sub-agents once. They were resumed from their saved state.
